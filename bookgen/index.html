The six laws (why this format works)
  1. Deadpan, present tense, third person. No exclamation marks. The narrator finds nothing unusual here.
  2. Specific numbers beat vague ones. Six headbands, not several. Eleven minutes, not barely any. Nothing lands on ten.
  3. The Observer never escalates. Her weapon is the mundane alternative — the thing he could do at home for free.
  4. The jargon must be real. Insiders laugh because it's accurate, outsiders because it's gibberish. Same line, both audiences.
  5. One sincere beat near the end. Chapter 9: "Camilla has never seen him so happy." This is why it's affectionate instead of cruel.
  6. Escalate, never resolve. Nobody learns. It only gets worse. End on VOLUME 1.
